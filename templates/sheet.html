5 · Method & provenance

  • Resampling. The 1-second tape is bucketed on wall-clock IST boundaries (epoch − epoch % tf). Open = first tick in the bucket, close = last tick, high/low = extremes, ticks = samples seen. The IST offset (19,800 s) divides exactly by 12/36/60/180/300, so every timeframe aligns to the IST clock.
  • Moving averages. Simple moving averages of the bar close at periods 2, 9 and 44, plotted together on the price pane -- the sheet's only pane. The period of each price line is editable in the toolbar; the analytics rows stay fixed at 2, 9 and 44.
  • Chart window. Every timeframe opens on its most recent 100 candles ([sheet] window_bars in the recipe) and sizes the bars to fill the pane, so the five panels open at the same readable scale. Drag or scroll right for the earlier bars and left back towards the latest. The window is a view, not a truncation: the arrows, the ten-row table and both exports cover the whole session. A double-click snaps a chart back to the latest 100.
  • Analytics table. Ten metric rows beneath the chart, one column per candle: Candle/Time, then SMA 2/9/44 Change (%), then SMA 2/9/44 Consecutive Count (+N / −N), then SMA 2 vs SMA 9 Direction Streak (+ABOVE / −BELOW), then the two crossing rows — SMA 2 vs SMA 9 Cross Direction and SMA 9 vs SMA 44 Distance (%) at SMA 2/SMA 9 Cross — and last SMA 9 vs SMA 44 (ABOVE / BELOW).
  • Percentage change. (current SMA − previous SMA) / previous SMA × 100, per candle and independent for each period. A candle whose previous SMA does not exist yet (the first n−1 candles) is left blank, and a zero previous value is guarded against division by zero.
  • Consecutive counts. Each row reports the length of the current unbroken run, not a running total: a rise extends the rising run and drops the falling run to 0; a fall does the mirror image; a reversal therefore starts a new run at +1 (or −1); a change of exactly zero resets both runs to 0. The three periods are counted independently, so a fresh rise after a fall reads +1 / −0.
  • Direction streak. The seventh row pairs SMA 2 with SMA 9. +n / −0 counts consecutive candles in which SMA 2 is above SMA 9 while SMA 9 is rising; +0 / −n counts consecutive candles in which SMA 2 is below SMA 9 while SMA 9 is falling. Only one side can ever be non-zero. A candle that satisfies neither rule — the two averages equal, or SMA 9's change rounding to 0.0000 — resets the row to +0 / −0, so a switch from BELOW to ABOVE (or the reverse) starts the new streak at 1.
  • Crossing rows. The eighth row names the candle on which SMA 2 swapped sides of SMA 9: ABOVE when SMA 2 was below SMA 9 on the previous candle and is above it now, BELOW for the mirror image, and empty when the pair stayed on the same side, either candle's two averages are equal, either average is unavailable, or it is the session's first candle — there is no previous candle to compare with. The ninth row prints the signed distance ((SMA 9 − SMA 44) / SMA 44) × 100 read on that crossing candle itself, and stays empty when there is no crossing, when either average is unavailable, or when SMA 44 is zero. Both rows are descriptive: they name where a change of side happened and how far the slow pair sat apart at that moment.
  • Relationship row. The tenth row prints which side of each other SMA 9 and SMA 44 sit on, one word per candle: ABOVE when SMA 9 is above SMA 44 on that candle, BELOW when it is below, and empty when the two are equal or SMA 44 has no value yet. Unlike the crossing row this is a comparison of the candle's own two averages, with no previous candle involved, so it holds on every candle both averages exist — it answers "which side are they on", not "when did they swap".
  • Green arrow. The first of the sheet's two marker rules. A green arrow is drawn below the bar on the price pane of every timeframe on the candles where all seven conditions hold — on the candle itself: the SMA 2 vs SMA 9 cross reading ABOVE, the SMA 9 vs SMA 44 distance (%) that cross carried inside 0.0000 – 0.0250 (both ends included), the SMA 9 vs SMA 44 relationship reading ABOVE, and the dashboard's minute the candle opens in reading pe_oi_change above ce_oi_change; and on the candle before it: the SMA 44 change (%) strictly above 0.0005, an SMA 9 consecutive count that is a falling run of 7 to 15, and an SMA 44 consecutive count that is a rising run of 7 or more. That is a seven-condition AND, not a score: one condition missing and no arrow is drawn, and the session's first candle can never carry one because it has no previous candle to read. The flow pair is the session's own dashboard column for that minute, not a sum or a difference the sheet computes — and a session that ships no dashboard has no such reading, so that one condition is not read for it rather than being read as a zero. Every threshold lives in the recipe's [signal] block and travels in the payload with the session. The rule runs on all five timeframes — a coarse tape may simply never satisfy the distance window, and then it shows 0 rather than nothing — each timeframe's arrows are counted in its panel chips and in the comparison matrix, the candle under the pointer reads its own arrow in the cursor badge, and the same flag ships as the green_arrow column (1 / 0) in the CSV and the JSON, so the drawn arrow and the exported number are one value. Nothing else in this sheet draws a marker or states a verdict.
  • Red arrow. The second of the sheet's two marker rules, and the exact mirror of the first. A red arrow is drawn above the bar on the price pane of every timeframe on the candles where all seven conditions hold the other way round — on the candle itself: the SMA 2 vs SMA 9 cross reading BELOW, the SMA 9 vs SMA 44 distance (%) that cross carried inside −0.0250 – 0.0000 (both ends included), the SMA 9 vs SMA 44 relationship reading BELOW, and the dashboard's minute the candle opens in reading ce_oi_change above pe_oi_change; and on the candle before it: the SMA 44 change (%) strictly below −0.0005, an SMA 9 consecutive count that is a rising run of 7 to 15, and an SMA 44 consecutive count that is a falling run of 7 or more. It is not a second set of numbers to keep in step: the recipe declares one rule, the green one, and this rule is derived from it — every word flipped, the distance window negated with its ends swapped, the change threshold negated, the two consecutive-count runs exchanged, and the two sides of the flow comparison swapped — so the red arrow is the green rule read the other way round and the two can never drift apart. A candle carries one arrow or the other and never both, because an ABOVE crossing and a BELOW one cannot happen on the same candle. Every value printed here travels in the payload's own redSignal block, so a reader can check the mirror against the signal block it inverts, number by number. [signal] red_arrow = false keeps the column, always 0, and drops the marks. The rule is counted on its own in the panel chips, the comparison matrix and the summary's redSignal funnel, the cursor badge reads its own line, and it ships as the red_arrow column (1 / 0) beside the green one in the CSV and the JSON.
  • CE / PE OI change. A session whose dashboard ships beside its tape — or on its own, since the feed's dashboard carries the session's per-second prices too — (NIFTY_<date>_DASHBOARD.json) also gets the fourth section: one chart drawn from the dashboard's own flow list, one row per minute, plotting that minute's ce_oi_change and pe_oi_change — the call and put open-interest change the feed reports for the minute, which can be negative, so the two lines read against a dashed zero line. They share one pane and one IST time axis with the candles — each timestamp is converted to the same epoch the bars carry — and the legend reads the minute under the pointer, printing the feed's two figures and nothing else. Both columns are plotted exactly as supplied: nothing is summed, differenced, resampled or smoothed, and the price chart stays the sheet's only price pane. A session that ships no dashboard file hides the section rather than drawing an empty chart, so the day strip can still move across sessions.
  • Crosshair. Hover anywhere on a timeframe for a live read-out of time, OHLC, change, all three averages, all three indicator changes, all three run lengths, the SMA 2 vs SMA 9 direction streak, the selected candle's cross direction with the SMA 9 vs SMA 44 distance it carried, and which side those two averages sit on; the matching candle column and ledger row highlight. Inside the chart, a cursor badge repeats the analytics families for the candle under the pointer.
  • Rebuild. python build_charts.py regenerates this sheet plus the CSV/JSON exports under output/data/.
